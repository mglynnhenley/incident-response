PART 1 — GTR TECHNICAL REVIEW ("Power Surge Disruption on 9th August 2019: Technical Review", dated 3 September 2019; provided by GTR to the ESO and appended to the ESO technical report as Appendix F)
G01  Event summary 1: from information supplied by the National Grid at 1653 [reported] on Friday 9 August, the frequency of the OLE AC voltage supply dropped below 49 Hz [reported] for 16 seconds [reported]; it dropped below 48.89 Hz [reported] to a minimum of 48.8 Hz [reported] for milliseconds.
G02  Event summary 2: there was no identified OLE AC voltage supply interruption.
G03  Event summary 3: all Desiro City Class 700 and 717 units operating on AC voltage suffered a Protective Shutdown, in which the converter known as the 4QC (4 Quadrant Controller) shut down. No other AC trains in the GTR fleet suffered power-related issues from the event (e.g. Class 387, Class 365, Class 313).
G04  Event summary 4: in passenger service this involved circa 60 [estimated] units, Class 700 FLU/RLUs and Class 717, suffering a 4QC lock out.
G05  Event summary 5: with the 4QC shut down the train switches to battery power: loss of HVAC (fan-only fresh air supply), standby reduced lighting (emergency lighting activates when battery voltage drops), no at-seat power, no PIS displays (audio only from the cab) and no traction power. The GSM-R radio remains active.
G06  Event summary 6: after failed attempts to repower the trains, the established first response for a train failing with these symptoms is for the driver to reboot the train, known as a Battery Reset, which takes approximately 10 minutes [estimated].
G07  Event summary 7: Fleet Control diagnosed the issue quickly and a global GSM-R call was broadcast instructing drivers to carry out the Battery Reset process.
G08  Event summary 8: after this, 27 [reported] of the affected units were recovered.
G09  Event summary 9: the remaining circa 30 [estimated] units required the Protective Shutdown to be unlocked by a technician with a laptop attending each unit.
G10  Event summary 10: 17 [reported] technicians were available at the time of the event; they were sent immediately to stranded units with laptops, and a further 24 [reported] technicians were mobilised within the next hour. The affected trains were widely spread geographically and some were not easily accessible.
G11  Event summary 11: the process took some time and resulted in 23 [reported] train evacuations and severe levels of service disruption.
G12  Cause 1: the Desiro City from Siemens Mobility is a software-enabled commuter train and requires protection against power supply frequency excursions, for safety reasons and to protect low power electronics.
G13  Cause 2: the Siemens technical specification for the train states that it will continue to operate with supply frequency drops down to 48.5 Hz [reported] for short periods of time.
G14  Cause 3: the NR Electrification System Compatibility document NR/GN/ELP/27010 ("Guidance for compatibility between electric trains and electrification systems"), a Manufacturing and Supply Agreement compliance requirement, identifies that the supply frequency can fall to 48.5 Hz [reported] in extreme conditions.
G15  Cause 4: a review of the Class 700 NoBo design submission (Siemens document A6Z00036309602 item 4.2.8.2.2) shows that compliance with both NR/GN/ELP 27010 and EN 50163 would be demonstrated.
G16  Cause 5: Siemens advised that the train's supply frequency response was designed to comply with EN 50163 Clause 4.2 Note 2, which permits train drives to disconnect at 49 Hz [reported]; using this value in the design led the train to shut down its drives protectively when the supply frequency fell below 49 Hz [reported].
G17  Cause 6: Siemens also clarified that there should not have been a Permanent Lockout following a protective shutdown caused by a supply frequency drop; all trains should have been recoverable by Battery Reset, whereas 30 [reported] trains were not recoverable. This was not the intended behaviour of the train.
G18  Cause 7: the affected Class 700 and 717 sets therefore did not react according to their design intent in these circumstances; the risk of this happening was not known before the power event on Friday 9 August.
G19  Cause 8: separately, the new TCMS (Train Control Management System) software version 3.27 removed the driver's ability to recover from a Permanent Lockout by using the Battery Reset process.
G20  Cause 9: on 9 August all units that required a technician to recover power were at software level 3.27 or above; the 28 [reported] units recovered by the driver performing a Battery Reset were at the previous TCMS software level of 3.25 or below.
G21  Conclusion 1: all Class 700 and Class 717 trains operating on AC suffered a Protective Shutdown of the 4QC controller because of a drop in the supply frequency below 49 Hz [reported] for 16 seconds [reported].
G22  Conclusion 2 to 4: this was not how the train system had been specified to operate, and the event should not have caused a Permanent Lockout fault. The effects were exacerbated because the fleet was undergoing a software change that removed the Battery Reset remedy for Permanent Lockout events, so the driver could not recover failed trains operating on the new software and a technician was required to attend.
G23  Planned mitigation 1: Siemens are developing a software patch to allow units that protectively shut down below 49 Hz [reported] supply frequency to recover themselves, without a reboot or laptop, when the frequency rises above 49.5 Hz [reported].
G24  Planned mitigation 2: neither Siemens nor GTR proposes to revert units to previous software versions, because of concerns about unit availability; based on discussions with the National Grid Head of Networks, the risk of a frequency excursion below 49 Hz [reported] before the patch is fully introduced is considered extremely unlikely.
G25  Planned mitigation 3: Siemens will also investigate how the train could be made to operate for a short time with the supply frequency falling to 48.5 Hz [reported].

PART 2 — ESO TECHNICAL REPORT (dated 6 September 2019): rail passages of section 5.2, the executive summary and section 7
E01  ESO 5.2: Class 700 and 717 trains shut down north of Farringdon and Kings Cross stations because their internal protection systems were triggered; the Network Rail overhead line power supply operated continually. The shutdown delayed all other trains behind them, requiring the temporary closure of London St Pancras and Kings Cross stations, which led to Friday rush-hour overcrowding.
E02  ESO 5.2, summarising GTR's report: all Desiro City Class 700 and Class 717 trains operating on AC power suffered a protective shutdown when the frequency deviation fell below 49 Hz [reported]. Of the approximately 60 [estimated] trains that shut down, half were restarted by the driver on site performing a reboot, which takes c. 10 minutes [estimated]; the remaining 30 [reported] trains required a technician to attend each train with a laptop to reset it.
E03  ESO 5.2: GTR stated that this was not how the train system had been specified to operate and that the event should not have caused a permanent lockout fault; the trains' technical specification states that they will continue to operate with supply frequency drops down to 48.5 Hz [reported] for short periods. All other GTR classes of train were unaffected.
E04  ESO 5.2: the effects were exacerbated because the fleet was undergoing a software change, which meant drivers could not recover trains operating on the new software. Siemens are developing a patch to let drivers recover the trains themselves without a reboot or a technician attending, and will investigate how the train could operate for a short time with a supply frequency of 48.5 Hz [reported].
E05  ESO 5.2: the impact on the rail network was 23 [reported] train evacuations, and thousands of passengers had their journeys delayed, with 371 [reported] trains cancelled, 220 [reported] part cancelled and 873 [reported] trains delayed. London St Pancras and King's Cross stations had to close for several hours due to overcrowding, and London Euston went exit only for a period of time.
E06  Executive summary: a major contributor to the rail disruption was a particular class of train operating in the South-East: approximately 60 [estimated] trains unexpectedly shut down when the frequency dropped below 49 Hz [reported], half of which required a visit from a technician to restart.
E07  ESO section 7 (report findings): disruption to rail services extended through Friday evening and into Saturday morning because approximately 30 [estimated] trains stopped operating and could not be restarted following the original frequency drop to 49 Hz [reported]; restarting them required a technician with a laptop to attend each train, which took some time.

PART 3 — ORR REPORT ("Report following railway power disruption on 9th August 2019", dated 3 January 2020)
O01  ORR 1.0: on Friday 9 August 2019 around 4:52pm [reported] a number of generators tripped off the power grid at approximately the same time. The frequency of the power supply to the rail network dropped from its nominal 50.0 Hz [reported] to below 49.0 Hz [reported] for approximately 33 seconds [estimated], falling to a low of 48.8 Hz [reported]. The majority of rolling stock operated without any issues.
O02  ORR 1.0: 31 [reported] trains were stranded as a result of the temporary drop in frequency. On the Class 700 and Class 717 rolling stock, protection systems operated within 200 ms [reported] of the frequency falling to 49.0 Hz [reported], causing a lock-out of 29 [reported] trains: 22 [reported] trains were permanently locked out and could not be reset without a technician attending on site, while 7 [reported] trains were restored through driver reset. Two Class 387 trains were also trapped as a result of the lock-out of the Class 700 and Class 717 trains. Passengers had to be evacuated (detrained) from 30 [reported] trains to a place of safety, and there were substantial knock-on delays following recovery of the vehicles.
O03  ORR 2.1: the supplied power frequency dropped from a nominal 50.0 Hz [reported] to a low of 48.8 Hz [reported] after 16 seconds [reported]; it remained below 49.0 Hz [reported] for approximately 33 seconds [estimated] and was restored to its nominal 50 Hz [reported] 3 minutes and 36 seconds [reported] later. ORR's Figure 1 (not reproduced here) is a frequency trace captioned as extracted from the National Grid technical report "published 06/08/2019".
O04  ORR 2.1: the majority of rolling stock operated without notable issues, but a fleet of Class 700 and Class 717 trains operated by GTR shut down within 200 ms [reported] of the frequency dropping below 49.0 Hz [reported].
O05  ORR 2.2: within 200 ms [reported] of the supply frequency dropping below 49.0 Hz [reported], 29 [reported] trains comprising Class 700 and Class 717, operated by GTR, shut down and were locked out by on-board safety systems. Initial attempts by drivers to bring the trains back into operation proved abortive, and Fleet Control had to advise drivers on the next action.
O06  ORR 2.2: ORR quotes GTR's technical review that all Desiro City Class 700 and 717 units operating on AC voltage suffered a Protective Shutdown in which the converter, known as the 4QC (expanded by ORR as Four Quadrant Chopper), shut down. When the 4QC shuts down the train is isolated from the traction power supply; only the limited on-board battery supply remains, which powers certain key systems but not traction.
O07  ORR 2.2: after Fleet Control diagnosed the shutdown, a GSM-R call was broadcast instructing drivers of affected trains to carry out the Battery Reset Process, which recovered 7 [reported] of the affected units; 22 [reported] trains could not be reset and required a technician to attend to recover them.
O08  ORR 2.2: at the time of the event too few technicians were available for the number of stranded trains; 18 [reported] technicians with laptops were sent to restore the trains immediately, and more technicians were mobilised within the next hour.
O09  ORR 2.3: some trains were in locations that were not easily accessible, which made recovery time-consuming. Passengers were safely detrained at stations and on track from 30 [reported] stranded trains. There were significant delays, cancellations and part-cancellations of train journeys; full service was restored within 24 hours [reported].

O11  ORR 2.5: delay compensation claims received by train operators in period 5 (21 July to 18 August) rose to the highest level since the May 2018 timetable disruption, with a particular peak for GTR; ORR states this cannot be directly attributed to the power disruption but is likely to have been a factor. There was no corresponding peak in complaints.
O12  ORR 3.0: ORR engaged with Network Rail, GTR and Siemens to understand why the power disruption significantly affected railway services despite traction power not being lost. Network Rail owns and manages the infrastructure; GTR operates the affected trains and carries out some of the maintenance; Siemens, the manufacturer, designed the software on the affected trains and also maintains some of the trains.
O13  ORR 3.1: ORR uses "lock-out" for a situation where a component's operation has been stopped by a software-based protection system. Permanent lock-outs protect against high-severity on-board risks such as fire and electrocution, and need a technician with a laptop to connect physically to the train to reset them. Temporary lock-outs protect against low-severity risks after a short-lived fault or mal-operation: the train resets itself once the fault clears, or the driver resets the software using a Battery Reset Process.
O14  ORR 3.1: all Class 700 and 717 trains had been programmed by Siemens to operate from a nominal 50.0 Hz [reported] supply with a minimum frequency of 49.0 Hz [reported]. When the software detected that the frequency had dropped below this level it operated as designed, disconnecting the traction drive within 200 ms [reported].
O15  ORR 3.1: individual trains had different software levels installed, as a software change was being implemented progressively across the fleets. On detecting the frequency drop, the units with software version 3.25.x went into a temporary lock-out while the units with version 3.27.x went into permanent lock-out; drivers could reset the temporary lock-outs with a battery reset, while the permanent lock-outs required a technician to attend.
O16  ORR 3.2: version 3.27.x was intended to address a safety risk identified with 3.25.x: the TCMS could lock out a train system in response to a serious fault or out-of-specification condition, only for the driver to clear it with a battery reset and restore power to the fault. Siemens therefore implemented in 3.27.x a permanent lock-out that a battery reset could not clear, needing a technician to analyse its cause before clearing it; among the trigger conditions selected was the detection of a low power supply frequency.
O17  ORR 3.3: the lock-out on out-of-limit supply frequency is understood to protect against electromagnetic interference from the 4QC in a range that can affect signalling circuits. ORR states that such protection is only required while the supply is outside the specified frequency range, so the lock-out could be permitted to reset automatically when the frequency returns to nominal, and that the service-disrupting implications of a lock-out needing a driver or technician reset appear not to have been given weight when the protection parameters for the on-train software were developed.
O18  ORR 3.3: variations in supply frequency affect many trains at once, with the same response from all trains on the same software. ORR states that the collective response of the Class 700 and 717 trains was in accordance with the software design but was not an explicit intention, and that Siemens accepts the temporary reduction in frequency should not have been considered a situation requiring a permanent lock-out.
O19  ORR 3.4: ORR quotes an interim Siemens email: in the original Class 700 design the 4QCs stop pulsing when the line frequency is out of range and restart automatically, without driver action, when it comes back in range; with the hysteresis implemented, the 4QCs lock when the line frequency drops below 49 Hz [reported] and restart when it rises above 49.5 Hz [reported].
O20  ORR 4.0 (conclusion): 23 [reported] trains were evacuated without incident. The significant delays could be attributed to the permanent lock-out of some of the Class 700 and Class 717 trains; findings so far show that the TCMS software classed the frequency reduction as a scenario requiring a permanent lock-out.
O21  ORR 4.0 (conclusion): the permanent lock-out resulted in 30 [reported] trains running the upgraded software being effectively stranded on the network and requiring a technician reset, with significant knock-on effects beyond the initial incident; full service was restored within 24 hours [reported].
O22  ORR 4.0 (conclusion): so far ORR has not been able to establish any non-compliance with the Railways (Interoperability) Regulations 2011, as amended, that could warrant enforcement action; the findings are lessons learned to be fed back for awareness and improvement.

PART 4 — RAIL FIGURES IN OTHER PUBLISHED REPORTS
X01  Ofgem report (dated 3 January 2020), 2.6: 29 [reported] GTR Class 700 and 717 trains shut down and became stranded when the system frequency fell below 49 Hz [reported], due to their own on-board automatic frequency protection systems; two Class 387 trains were also trapped by the stranded units, and altogether passengers had to be safely evacuated from 30 [reported] trains.
X02  Ofgem 2.7: some protection settings were reset by the driver, but 22 [reported] permanently locked out and had to be reset by a technician, which was the main cause of disruption to rail lines into St Pancras International and King's Cross. A software update is being introduced so that these trains can be reset automatically if the frequency drops below 49 Hz [reported] in future.
X03  Ofgem 2.9: there was significant disruption to the rail network, with 371 [reported] services cancelled and 220 [reported] part cancelled. Ofgem executive summary: rail services were particularly affected, with more than 500 [reported] services disrupted.
X04  E3C interim report: the Department for Transport confirmed that a safety mechanism built into all operational Class 700 and Class 717 Desiro City trains detected the frequency drop and triggered 60 [reported] trains to shut down; around half were restarted by their drivers and the rest required engineers to be dispatched, blocking tracks into St Pancras International and King's Cross. 371 [reported] services were cancelled and 873 [reported] delayed, and St Pancras International, King's Cross and Euston had to limit passenger numbers entering the stations at various times.
X05  E3C interim report: a number of trains did not end up in their correct location at the end of the day, which disrupted some early morning services on Saturday 10 August; the executive summary states that rail delays extended into Sunday 11 August. The Class 700 and 717 trains had been introduced over the last three years.
X06  E3C final report (January 2020), section 4: Thameslink Class 700 and 717 train systems were overprotective to frequency drops and shut down; a number of trains required manual engineer intervention to restart, and the complexity of the restart process caused significant disruption: 371 [reported] cancelled services, 220 [reported] part cancelled services and 873 [reported] delayed services.
