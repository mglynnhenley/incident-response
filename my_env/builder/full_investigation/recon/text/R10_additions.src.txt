SECTION F — GENERATOR FAULT RIDE THROUGH AND VOLTAGE CONTROL (as the reports describe them)
F1   Ofgem 3.24: the Grid Code requirements on generators' responses to network faults (Fault Ride Through) require that generators remain connected and transiently stable following a fault on the transmission system, with power output recovering to at least 90% [reported] within 0.5 seconds [reported]; Ofgem's footnote cites Connection Condition CC.6.3.15.1.
F2   Ofgem 3.24: the Grid Code requires an overall voltage control system which appropriately dampens or limits swings; Ofgem's footnote cites Connection Condition CC.A.7.2.5.2, Voltage Control (Oscillations).
F3   Ofgem 2.12: transmission-connected generators have a requirement to remain connected and continue providing power to the system following faults on the transmission network.
F4   ESO 2.4: generators that use the transmission networks are signatories to the CUSC, which places an obligation on them to meet the requirements of the Grid Code, including any site-specific criteria in their connection agreement. The Grid Code sets out the capabilities a generator must have, including voltage control and frequency control, and states what a generator is expected to do during and after a network fault. While an offshore network and generation connection is being developed, the project is treated in the same way as a generator (Offshore Transmission System Development User Works, OTSDUW) and is subject to Grid Code requirements.
F5   E3C final: generators connecting to the transmission network are required to comply with the Connection and Use of System Code and the Grid Code, which set out the range of capabilities a generator must have, including voltage control and frequency control, and what a generator is expected to do during and after a fault on the network.

SECTION G — GENERATOR COMPLIANCE PROCESS (as the ESO describes it)
G1   ESO 2.4: the Grid Code sets out an extensive process for new generators to demonstrate that they can meet certain requirements to enable safe connection, detailed in its CP (Compliance Process) section; it is for the generators to assure themselves of their compliance with the Grid Code requirements.
G2   ESO App. G: generators are responsible for demonstrating and maintaining compliance with the Grid Code, both when connecting initially and on an ongoing basis, through a combination of studies, simulations and testing. The ESO runs the compliance process; for the initial connection of any large or directly connected generator, the generator proceeds through stage gates (energisation stage, interim operational stage and final operation stage), providing the required compliance evidence at each, and the ESO issues a notice to proceed through the stage gate if compliance has been met.
G3   ESO App. G: a generator that makes configuration changes which may affect compliance is responsible for first notifying the ESO of the change and then demonstrating compliance; the ESO is not responsible for checking compliance on an ongoing basis. If a generator believes it would require more than 84 days [reported] to resolve compliance issues, the ESO issues a Limited Operational Notice (LON) and works with it to achieve full compliance.
G4   ESO App. G, footnote: the Grid Code defines a large generator as greater than 99.9 MW [reported] in England & Wales, greater than 9.9 MW [reported] in Scottish Hydro Electricity Transmission, greater than 29.9 MW [reported] in the Scottish Power region and 9.9 MW [reported] offshore.
G5   ESO App. D glossary: EON, Energisation Operational Notification, is the first milestone in the commissioning process; ION (Part A), Interim Operational Notification for dynamic reactive compensation on OFTO assets, the second milestone; ION (Part B), Interim Operational Notification to export active power, the second or third milestone; FON, Final Operational Notification, the last milestone.
G6   ESO answer 45: there are no direct tests of generator ride-through for faults and large frequency changes, as these would require an intentional fault on the transmission system; the asset owner performs simulation studies, and the ESO will not issue an Interim Operational Notice until it has reviewed these studies and determined that they are in line with Grid Code requirements.

SECTION H — DEMAND RECONNECTION AFTER LFDD (in addition to D9)
H1   Ofgem 3.43: Ofgem describes reconnecting sites "without being told to do so by the ESO" as not appearing to have technically met the requirements of the Grid Code; its footnote cites Grid Code Operation Code 6.6.4.

SECTION I — INCIDENT NOTIFICATION AND COMMUNICATION (as the reports describe it)
I1   ESO answer 34: current ESO procedure states that, following an event of loss of supply, a clear communication to Ofgem and BEIS is essential and is recommended to be initiated as soon as possible, in line with the 'Energy Incident Reporting' requirement set by Ofgem dated December 2017; resolution of the incident remains the primary objective in all cases.
I2   ESO answer 37: where GB Transmission System Warnings apply to system conditions or events with widespread effect, the ESO notifies Users by fax in accordance with the Grid Code; Control Telephony is used for instructing Demand Control. The ENCC Power System Manager and duty officer hold an SO communications document with contact details for BEIS and Ofgem duty officers, TO and DNO control room managers, generation and supply companies, and media and escalation contacts.

SECTION J — LFDD AND ESSENTIAL SERVICES (as the reports describe them)
J1   E3C final, chapter 3: the primary purpose of LFDD is to stabilise the system, so the Grid Code contains no requirement for DNOs to afford particular groups of customers, for example essential services, any form of protection; DNOs have some discretion in how they disconnect demand, largely dictated by the architecture of the system.
J2   Ofgem 3.41: there is no obligation on DNOs to refrain from disconnecting essential service providers in their LFDD requirements; LFDD equipment does not operate at an individual site level, and individual customers operating essential services should maintain and operate back-up power supplies.
J3   ESO 5.3: the ESO report refers to Protected Sites under the terms of the Electricity Supply Emergency Code (ESEC) and records that a site may request its DNO to categorise it as a Protected Site under ESEC.
J4   ORR 2.4: 48.8 Hz [reported] is one of the LFDD bands at which the DNO sheds load to stabilise the system and to protect essential services such as the rail traction supply; it is the duty holder's responsibility to ensure backup systems are in place where loss of power may lead to emergency situations (for example generators, uninterruptable power supplies or specific contracts with the DNO).

SECTION K — RAIL TRACTION SUPPLY FREQUENCY (as the reports describe it)
K1   ORR 1.0 and 2.1: Railway Group Standard GL/RT1210 (AC Energy Subsystem and Interfaces to Rolling Stock Subsystem) and BS EN 50163:2004 (Railway applications — Supply voltages of traction systems) specify a lower frequency limit of 47.0 Hz [reported] for a traction power supply.
K2   ORR 2.1: an advisory note to the standard defines a narrower range of 49.0 Hz [reported] to 51.0 Hz [reported] within which the train is required to operate normally; outside this range it is permitted to reduce performance or to disconnect the vehicle drives.
K3   ORR 3.4: the trains were introduced using the interoperability process imposed by the Railways (Interoperability) Regulations 2011, which requires compliance with Technical Specifications for Interoperability (TSI); BS EN 50163:2004 is mandated by the Energy TSI. ORR quotes its Note 2 (BS EN 50163:2004+A1:2007 Clause 4.2): vehicles will operate only within frequency tolerances of 49 Hz [reported] to 51 Hz [reported] in the 25 kV [reported] / 50 Hz [reported] range, and outside this range vehicle performance may be reduced or the vehicle drives disconnected.
K4   GTR technical review (ESO App. F), cause 3: the NR Electrification System Compatibility document NR/GN/ELP/27010 ("Guidance for compatibility between electric trains and electrification systems"), a Manufacturing and Supply Agreement compliance requirement, identifies that the supply frequency can fall to 48.5 Hz [reported] in extreme conditions.
K5   Ofgem 2.7: traction supply standards in the rail sector specify a lower operating limit of 47 Hz [reported], but also refer to a narrower range of 49 Hz [reported] to 51 Hz [reported] within which trains are required to operate normally; outside this range they are permitted to reduce their performance or disconnect.
