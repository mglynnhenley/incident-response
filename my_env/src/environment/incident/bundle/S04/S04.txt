PART 1 — SHIFT NARRATIVE (reconstructed from published accounts)
Plant state before the disturbance
N1  The station (740 MW [reported] CCGT, connected at Eaton Socon 400 kV [reported] substation) had
    all three units operating: gas turbines GT1A and GT1B and steam turbine ST1C.
Steam turbine trip
N2  At the start of the station's event sequence the 110 V AC UPS -B- and then UPS -A- raised general
    fault alarms (station sequence-of-events record).
N3  Within 1 second [reported] of the lightning strike and the resulting transmission system disturbance,
    steam turbine ST1C tripped while generating 244 MW [reported].
Steam bypass operation
N4  With the steam turbine lost, the plant went into steam bypass mode: steam generated from the gas
    turbines is fed directly into the condenser. This is the normal operation following loss of the
    steam turbine, intended to allow sustained operation of the gas turbines.
N5  Following the steam turbine trip, the pressure in the steam system rose.
First gas turbine trip
N6  Around a minute [reported] after the transmission system fault, gas turbine GT1A (generating
    210 MW [reported]) tripped automatically due to a high-pressure excursion in the steam bypass system.
Second gas turbine trip
N7  The steam system pressure continued to rise after the GT1A trip, and the plant operators made the
    decision to manually trip the other operational gas turbine due to safety risks.
N8  Around 30 seconds [reported] after the GT1A trip, GT1B (187 MW [reported]) was manually tripped by
    the operating staff in response to high steam pressures. Total station loss: 641 MW [reported].

PART 2 — POST-EVENT ANNOTATIONS (attributed, in each source's order; not part of the shift record)
Annotation A — operator report "RWE Preliminary Findings Regarding the Event on 9th August 2019",
dated 30 August 2019:
A1  UPS: "The investigation to date has found that all UPS' at Site have functioned correctly to enable
    continuity of supply to plant equipment." "Therefore, we do not now believe the UPS' were related
    to the subsequent trip."
A2  UPS: "RWE's hypothesis is that the UPS' have initiated a changeover to battery back-up supplies as a
    consequence of the system disturbance. The exact characteristic of the power supply to the UPS
    causing it to switch to battery operation is still to be determined and subject to further
    investigation."
A3  UPS: OEM resilience testing requested during the outage commencing 6 September 2019 [reported].
A4  Steam turbine: "Our investigations have confirmed that the Steam Turbine tripped due to a
    discrepancy in the speed signals after the point of the Transmission System fault clearance."
    A comprehensive investigation of hardware, software, fault handling and diagnostic coverage for
    the conditions the steam turbine was subjected to during this rare system disturbance is ongoing.
A5  Gas turbines: "For reasons presently unknown a high-pressure excursion occurred on GT1A resulting in
    an automated trip. Given the prevailing conditions all systems functioned as expected. GT1B was
    manually tripped shortly after as a result of excessive steam pressure."
A6  Gas turbines: "A physical inspection of the bypass system is now planned during the forthcoming
    outage in September 2019 to help determine the root cause of the pressure excursions."
Annotation B — regulator report, Ofgem "9 August 2019 power outage report", dated 3 January 2020:
B1  3.28: "We have established that the steam turbine tripped initially because of a discrepancy in the
    three [reported] independent speed sensors on the turbine. This discrepancy exceeded the tolerance
    of the control system, causing the generator to automatically shut down. The root cause of the
    discrepancy in the speed sensors has not been established."
B2  3.29: following the steam turbine trip the steam system pressure rose; safety systems automatically
    shut down one of the gas turbines due to excessive pressure in the steam bypass; as the pressure kept
    rising, the plant operators manually tripped the other gas turbine due to safety risks. "The root
    cause of these high pressure conditions has not been established."
B3  3.29: "Given the sequence of events and in absence of an established root cause, it is our
    preliminary view that the steam turbine's anomalous speed readings and the resulting trip were due
    to the transmission network fault following the lightning strike."
