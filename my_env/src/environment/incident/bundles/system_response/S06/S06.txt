PART 1 — DNO RETURNS TO THE ESO DATA REQUEST (as described in the ESO technical report dated
6 September 2019, section 4.2)
R1   After the event ESO contacted all DNOs and requested data on the volume of embedded generation that
     was disconnected or changed output during the event, and asked them to indicate the reason for the
     change of output, for example RoCoF, Vector Shift or Active Network Management (ANM) operation.
R2   All DNOs responded and indicated a combined total of 462 MW [reported] of embedded generation lost
     during the event.
R3   Some DNOs noted challenges in obtaining the data due to the way it is collected or stored. Without
     confirmation from generators, DNOs were unable to determine whether a specific generator tripped due
     to RoCoF or Vector Shift.
R4   A number of embedded generators and demand customers advised that their protection operated when
     the frequency reached 49 Hz [reported] and as such they were disconnected from the system.

PART 2 — LIMITS OF THE AVAILABLE DATA (as stated in the reports)
L1   ESO answers to E3C questions (Appendix M, data available on 28 August 2019): the majority of
     distribution connected generation provides no indication of proposed output, and no actual output
     data to the ESO.
L2   Ofgem 3.45: most DNOs only record information on generation volumes on their networks on a
     half-hourly basis, obscuring shorter-term events; the data DNOs provided gives only a partial view of
     which distributed generators tripped off or reduced output, so volumes lost at different locations
     and their causes cannot be ascertained with confidence from it.
L3   Ofgem 3.37: the information DNOs collect and record on distributed generation is variable or
     severely limited; the exact causes and timeline of the incident cannot be fully established.
L4   Ofgem, footnote 19: it has not been possible to fully validate the ESO estimate with the DNOs, as
     they also do not monitor output from distributed generators in real-time.
L5   Ofgem, footnote 31: Ofgem ascertained the distributed generation losses in section 2 of its report
     based on its validation of the frequency event modelling carried out by the ESO.

E7   section 4.2: a number of embedded generators and demand customers advised that their protection

E12  Appendix I: based on the DNO data the RoCoF volume was 350 MW [estimated]; analysis of the simulation

ESO answers to E3C questions (Appendix M; data available on 28 August 2019)
E14  Answer 7: approximately 500 MW [estimated] of embedded generation was lost in the incident, estimated
     from the change in flows between the transmission and distribution networks in metered data from the
     ESO's IEMS system, corrected for the frequency characteristics of demand and for frequency response
     services in the distribution network; further analysis was to determine whether a split between RoCoF
     and vector shift could be made.
E3C interim report (October 2019)

Ofgem report dated 3 January 2020
E16  2.4.6: the frequency fell at a rate of change of frequency (RoCoF) above 0.125 Hz/s [reported]; some
     distributed generators have loss of mains protection triggered by RoCoF set at this rate; as a
     result, an estimated 350-430 MW [estimated] of distributed generation tripped off unnecessarily,
     based on information provided by the ESO.
E17  footnote 19 (to 2.4.6): this has been inferred by the ESO from a 500 MW [estimated] increase in
     transformer loadings at the interface with the distribution network; the ESO estimated
     150 MW [estimated] of vector shift and attributed the remainder to RoCoF; further ESO modelling
     suggests the total from vector shift and RoCoF could have been up to 580 MW [modelled].
E18  2.4.11 and footnote 20: there was a further net reduction in generation of 200 MW [reported] at
     49 Hz [reported]; some distributed generators tripped due to protection set to activate when the
     frequency falls to 49 Hz [reported], which could be the cause; the figure is net of demand
     disconnection, such as trains and large industrial customers.
E19  2.4.12-2.4.13: after system frequency dropped below 48.8 Hz [reported], triggering Stage 1 of LFDD,
     the ESO reported that the net demand reduction seen by the transmission system was only
     350 MW [reported]; this indicates that approximately 550 MW [estimated] of additional distributed
     generation was lost at this point.
E20  3.30: lower bound for total estimated distributed generation lost across the event is
     1,300 MW [estimated], and the loss could be as high as 1,500 MW [estimated].
E21  3.31: at least 500 MW [estimated] of distributed generation is estimated to have been lost due to
     loss of mains protection settings (RoCoF and vector shift) in the first second after the fault; some
     generators with capacities greater than 5 MW [reported] were reported to have de-loaded during the
     event and the cause is still to be determined precisely.
E22  3.32 and footnote 27: in excess of 200 MW [reported] of distributed generation tripped off when
     system frequency reached 49 Hz [reported]; significantly more may have tripped at this point, as the
     modelling is net of demand that was simultaneously disconnected such as the trains.
E3C final report (January 2020)
E23  section 1: five significant modes of embedded generator loss: vector shift, which disconnected
     approximately 150 MW [estimated]; RoCoF protection, which disconnected between 350-430 MW [reported];
     embedded generation shutting down at a frequency of 49 Hz [reported], not expected, resulting in a
     further net reduction of 200 MW [reported]; an unexpected increase in electricity demand in parallel
     with the loss of generation, attributed to the de-loading or tripping of 100 MW [reported] of
     embedded generation, the cause unknown at present; and the final loss, as reported in the ESO
     technical report, of 550 MW [reported], when the LFDD scheme was triggered. In total across the
     event, the loss of embedded generation is estimated to be in the range of
     1300 MW to 1500 MW [estimated].
