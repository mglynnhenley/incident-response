PART 3 — DNO STATEMENTS AS PUBLISHED
G1   ESO technical report, Table 1 (source column: DNOs): 16:53:49.398 [reported] Frequency breaches
     48.8 Hz [reported] trigger level resulting in LFDD. 931 MW [reported] of demand is automatically
     disconnected.
G2   ESO technical report, section 4.3: the DNOs have indicated that 931 MW (3.2%) [reported] of demand
     was automatically disconnected by the operation of the LFDD scheme.
G3   E3C interim report: post event, two [reported] DNOs reported that a number of LFDD stage 1 schemes
     did not trigger, and therefore did not disconnect demand. The report gives two possible reasons:
     the system frequency did not stay at 48.8 Hz [reported] long enough to trigger the operation of all
     LFDD schemes; or the +/-0.01 Hz [reported] tolerance setting on the LFDD schemes, which meant that
     when the system frequency fell to 48.792 Hz [reported], not all schemes were triggered.
G4   E3C interim report: disconnections were also experienced in Scotland, where LFDD stage 1 schemes are
     meant to be triggered when the system frequency falls to 48.5 Hz [reported]; this was confirmed to
     be due to incorrect LFDD settings that have subsequently been corrected.
G5   E3C final report (January 2020): the DNOs have indicated that 4.0% [reported] of demand was actually
     disconnected, equating to over 1 million [reported] customers.

PART 4 — POST-EVENT ANNOTATIONS (attributed; added after the event, not return content)
Annotation A — ESO technical report dated 6 September 2019, section 4.3:
A1   The LFDD process worked largely as expected. While the process provided slightly lower than
     5% [reported] of demand, this did not materially impact the function of the LFDD in returning the
     frequency to normal operational parameters.
Annotation B — Ofgem report dated 3 January 2020:
B1   2.4.12: frequency dropping below 48.8 Hz [reported] triggered DNOs to disconnect approximately
     5% [reported] of demand (Stage 1 of LFDD); 892 MW [reported] of net demand was disconnected in
     total from the local distribution networks.
B2   2.5: in total 892 MW [reported] of net demand was disconnected as a result of LFDD, representing
     approximately 4% [reported] of national demand and affecting 1.15 million [reported] customers.
B3   3.39: on average, the DNOs disconnected an estimated 4% [estimated] of demand as measured prior to
     the event, and some disconnected significantly less than 5% [reported].
B4   3.40: the principal cause of the lower levels of demand disconnection appears to be the technical
     specification of some LFDD relays which prevented them from activating; these relays would have
     activated if the frequency had dropped marginally lower, and the Grid Code permits this margin of
     error. Another cause may have been the disconnection of significant volumes of distributed
     generation as part of the LFDD operation, which lowered the net demand reduction.
B5   3.36: some DNOs disconnected distributed generation via LFDD that was providing either frequency
     response or reserve services.
B6   3.43: Eastern Power Networks plc and South Eastern Power Networks plc (both owned by UK Power
     Networks) reconnected sites without being told to do so by the ESO, and acknowledge that they do
     not appear to have technically met the requirements of the Grid Code (Ofgem cites Grid Code
     Operation Code 6.6.4); Ofgem states these instances were caused by the configuration of the DNOs'
     operational systems.
B7   3.44: SP Distribution erroneously disconnected 22 MW [reported] in Scotland when system frequency
     fell below 48.8 Hz [reported] and reconnected customers without informing the ESO; the
     disconnection occurred due to an incorrect setting in the DNO's LFDD equipment.
Annotation C — E3C final report (January 2020):
C1   Approximately 900 MW [reported] of demand was disconnected by LFDD stage one, shared out across the
     different DNOs in England and Wales.
C2   The E3C writes that stage one should have resulted in around 5% [reported] of national demand
     being disconnected, while the DNOs indicated 4.0% [reported]. Reasons it gives: the 5% [reported]
     demand figure is based on forecasted peak demand during an average cold spell; the frequency did
     not drop below 48.8 Hz [reported] for long enough to trigger the relays to operate; and the growth
     of embedded generation connected to the distribution network.
