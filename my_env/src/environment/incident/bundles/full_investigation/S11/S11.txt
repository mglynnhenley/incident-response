PART 1 — RAIL AND TRANSPORT POWER SUPPLIES
P01  ESO technical report (dated 6 September 2019), section 5.2: the DNOs confirmed that no track supplies
     were lost due to the DNOs' LFDD protection operation. Through its investigation, Network Rail stated that
     its supply disruption was likely to have been caused by supply interruption from the DNO networks, and is
     to undertake discussions with the DNOs to explore this further.
P02  ESO section 5.2: the built-in resilience of Network Rail's electrical power infrastructure meant traction
     power was maintained to the vast majority of the railway throughout the incident; supply-related trips
     occurred at two DC traction locations, which Network Rail is investigating further. Eight signal power
     supplies, principally in rural locations, suffered minor outages with minimal passenger impact; Network
     Rail is reviewing resilience at these locations with the DNOs.
P03  ESO section 5.2: London Underground confirmed impacts on the Victoria Line, which was suspended as a
     result of the event; service was restored at 17:35 [reported]. UKPN confirmed that LFDD did not affect
     the Victoria line; the UKPN control room contacted London Underground, which confirmed that it did not
     lose power supplies at any stations but had an internal traction issue.
P04  ORR report (dated 3 January 2020), sections 1.0 and 2.4: there was a traction power outage on the Wirral
     line of Merseyrail after rectifiers tripped when domestic supply was lost to LFDD, resulting in
     428 minutes [reported] of delay; in other areas no traction power outage was reported.
P05  ORR section 2.4: the substations at Tattenham and Chipstead experienced power quality issues, which
     caused the protective devices for the traction rectifiers to operate, temporarily switching the power
     off; the rectifiers were quickly reinstated, and traction power supply was maintained during this time.
P06  ORR section 2.4: two DNO supply points were lost, at Chatham and Lewes; ORR states this is still under
     investigation and may have been caused by LFDD.
P07  ORR section 2.4: eight signalling power supplies were lost in rural locations fed by local DNO networks
     that may have been affected by LFDD: Wye (Kent), Southease (Sussex), Norwood Fork (London), Trowbridge
     (Wiltshire), Magor (Wales), Bangor (Wales), Croes Newydd (Wales) and Kirby Thore (Cumbria).
     Investigations are ongoing into whether the power disruption led to the loss of these supplies.
P08  ORR section 4.0 (conclusion): Network Rail's electrical system reacted well although some resilience
     issues were identified; Network Rail has begun work to ensure adequate back-up supplies for non-traction
     essential supplies where necessary and is working with the DNO to improve resilience.
P09  Ofgem report (dated 3 January 2020), 2.8: the traction supplies to the Wirral line on Merseyrail were
     disconnected as a result of SP Energy Networks' LFDD operations. Three Transport for London stations and
     eight signalling sites at rural locations across England and Wales were also thought to have been
     affected by LFDD operations, although traction supplies were unaffected.
P10  E3C final report (January 2020), section 4: power supplies to the local Wirral Line were interrupted by
     the LFDD scheme, causing a 428 minute [reported] delay for passengers. Three Transport for London
     stations were disconnected as a result of LFDD operations; traction supplies to these sites were
     maintained, with minimal impact on passengers.
P11  E3C final report, section 4: eight rail signalling assets were affected, principally in rural locations
     across England and Wales, with minimal customer impact; 2 [reported] were disconnected by the LFDD
     scheme, with investigations ongoing into the cause for the remaining six. Two railway traction
     substations in the South of England were disrupted by the drop in frequency; they automatically switched
     to back-up generation with no impact on customer services.

PART 2 — HOSPITALS
H01  ESO section 5.3: Ipswich Hospital internal protection operated coincident with the timeframe of the
     lightning strike. Time-stamped data from UKPN shows that the hospital's load reduced by half in a period
     of 14 seconds [reported].
H02  ESO section 5.3: UKPN confirmed that the hospital was not part of its LFDD protection zone and that LFDD
     did not affect the substations supplying the hospital.
H03  ESO executive summary: impacts on other critical facilities included Ipswich hospital, which lost power
     due to the operation of its own protection systems.
H04  E3C interim report: a DNO confirmed that two hospitals were affected by LFDD, with their back-up
     generation working as designed. Another hospital was affected by the frequency fall and voltage excursion
     despite not being disconnected by LFDD; this was due to incorrect protection settings on the hospital's
     own network, which switched the site over to back-up generation, and one of its 11 [reported] generators
     failed to operate.
H05  E3C final report, section 4: four hospitals were impacted by the power outage; two were disconnected by
     the LFDD scheme and a further two by their internal safety systems. All hospitals automatically switched
     to back-up power supplies to ensure continued operation.
H06  Ofgem 2.11: two hospitals were disconnected as part of the LFDD and their back-up generators were
     activated; the E3C has reported that two other hospitals were disconnected by their own safety systems
     but switched to back-up generation.

PART 3 — AIRPORTS
A01  ESO section 5.3: Newcastle Airport is connected to the NPG network. NPG confirmed that the airport was
     disconnected as a result of the LFDD operation and that the LFDD scheme operated by NPG worked as
     planned; NPG is nonetheless reviewing all LFDD allocations. As far as NPG is aware, no Protected Sites
     under the Electricity Supply Emergency Code (ESEC) were affected by the incident.
A02  ESO section 5.3: Newcastle International Airport lost supplies from the network for 18 minutes [reported]
     between 16:53 [reported] and 17:11 [reported]. NPG indicated that the airport's Uninterruptable Power
     Supply (UPS) and standby generator resilience arrangements for its essential services operated smoothly.
A03  ESO section 5.3: on 12 August 2019 Northern Powergrid (Northeast) Limited received a request from
     Newcastle International Airport to be categorised as a Protected Site under ESEC, and it has now been
     registered as such.
A04  ESO executive summary: Newcastle airport was disconnected by the Low Frequency Demand Disconnection
     scheme.
A05  E3C interim report: DNOs confirmed that two airports, one in the Midlands and one in the north of
     England, were impacted. One was impacted as a result of LFDD stage 1 being automatically triggered; it
     had its own standby generation for its safety-critical systems, and power was restored via the
     distribution network after 17 minutes [reported]. The second, unaffected by LFDD, switched to back-up
     power supplies without issue and was restored within a few minutes; a fault with its on-site internal
     network meant that power to some services (check-in hall, central security search, hold baggage
     screening, and some telephony and comms systems) was delayed for up to 50 minutes [reported].
A06  E3C final report, section 4: Newcastle airport was disconnected from the electricity network following
     the LFDD and used back-up generation. The second airport was not impacted by the LFDD but switched to
     back-up power supplies and was restored within a few minutes; a fault with the on-site internal network
     disrupted some services, such as the check-in hall, for ~50 minutes [estimated].
A07  Ofgem 2.10: Newcastle airport was disconnected as part of LFDD; the E3C has reported that standby
     generation was in place and power was restored within minutes at Newcastle airport, and that a second
     airport switched to its back-up power supplies without issue although it was not affected by LFDD.

PART 4 — WATER
W01  E3C interim report: approximately 3,000 [estimated] people experienced water supply disruptions because
     booster water pumping stations failed to switch over automatically to back-up power supplies. Some would
     have had a temporary loss of running water at home, while others were unaffected because storage in the
     system allowed running water to continue; the majority of customers were restored within
     30 minutes [reported].
W02  E3C final report, section 4: the water sector experienced minimal impacts. Two water treatment works were
     disconnected, one through the LFDD scheme and one as a result of the company's internal safety systems.
     One water company reported a reduction in water pressure to 3000 [reported] customers because booster
     water pumping stations failed to switch automatically to back-up power; this was reduced to
     c.180 [estimated] customers within 30 minutes [reported].
W03  Ofgem 2.11: some water pumping stations were disconnected as part of the LFDD; the E3C has reported that
     their back-up supplies were not connected automatically and, as a result, several thousand customers
     experienced some disruption to their running water supply.

PART 5 — OIL REFINERY, CHEMICALS AND GAS
F01  E3C interim report: an oil refinery confirmed that it was disconnected as a result of the site's system,
     which detected a drop in frequency and disconnected the plant to protect on-site equipment. The refinery
     operations team used the site's emergency procedures and automated systems to shut down portions of the
     plant safely; because restarting large process units is complex, it took a few weeks to restore normal
     operations.
F02  E3C final report, section 4: wider industry reported the disconnection of an oil refinery and a chemicals
     manufacturing plant due to internal safety systems detecting the drop in frequency; to protect the assets
     both plants safely shut down operations, and it took several weeks for the refinery to resume full
     operations.
F03  E3C interim report: the gas networks and transmission operator reported that they implemented their
     response mitigation plans in a timely way.

PART 6 — ATTRIBUTED ANNOTATIONS ON ESSENTIAL SERVICES (statements made after the event)
N01  E3C final report, section 4: there is currently no agreed definition of what constitutes an essential
     service in this context; the report uses the term for organisations that rely on electricity to provide
     important daily services as well as critical industries, for example hospitals, airports, oil refineries
     and rail services.
N02  E3C final report, section 4: some essential services were disrupted by being disconnected directly
     through the LFDD mechanism; others by their automatic safety systems reacting unexpectedly to the
     frequency and voltage fluctuations, or by insufficient business continuity measures. Several sectors did
     not resume operations for several days.
N03  Ofgem 2.5: some essential service providers were directly disconnected as part of Stage 1 of the LFDD and
     disrupted because their back-up power supply arrangements were not effective; other services were
     affected indirectly because the providers' own safety systems were configured to disconnect automatically
     when system frequency fell.
N04  Ofgem 3.41 to 3.42: several essential service providers (such as hospitals and airports) were
     disconnected as part of LFDD; the LFDD equipment does not operate at an individual site level, so
     isolating particular sites is generally unfeasible. Ofgem states that disruptions at the sites of
     essential service providers directly affected were generally caused by failures in their back-up power
     arrangements, and that DNOs have significantly different approaches to evaluating which areas to
     disconnect during Stage 1 of the LFDD.

     interrupting electricity to outpatient and x-ray areas; business-continuity plans were invoked and the
