B1   The Frequency Simulation Engine (FSE) was used to infer the imbalance between supply and demand that
     would produce the actual frequency curve; the output of the frequency response services was then
     removed, leaving the changes in underlying supply and demand required to obtain the trace.
B2   ESO: "An event such as this is complex with many different things happening and so it is unlikely
     that we can explain all of the movements in the imbalance."

SECTION D — RESPONSE PLANNING ASSUMPTIONS AND DELIVERY ASSESSMENTS
D1   ESO Appendix H: the FSE adopts a pessimistic approach: the starting frequency is set as
     49.9 Hz [reported] for low frequency response calculation (in real time it would generally be
     kept higher), and response services are modelled at their minimum performance requirement.
D2   ESO answer 18 (data available on 28 August 2019): when calculating the volume of frequency response
     required to secure the largest loss, a 90% [reported] delivery rate is assumed.
D3   ESO answer 17, table note: demand varies with frequency; for 29 GW [reported] demand this effect is
     approximately 350 MW [estimated] at 49.5 Hz [reported], and it is unrelated to LFDD.
D4   ESO technical report, section 4.4: conservative modelling assumptions are employed to ensure there
     is contingency if not all contracted response delivers; overall response performance on 9 August
     was broadly in line with these planning assumptions, with some under-performance being followed up.
D5   ESO answer 27: in the days immediately after the event the ESO held 100 MW [reported] more frequency
     response whilst it analysed the incident, and has since removed this extra holding.
D6   Ofgem 3.6: there was only a narrow margin for error in securing the system against
     transmission-connected generator losses alone, and a high level of sensitivity to small changes in
     key assumptions; the ESO's processes for estimating the impact of distributed generation on
     requirements do not appear sufficiently robust.
D7   Ofgem 3.9: primary response providers under-delivered by 17% [reported] and secondary response
     providers by 14% [reported]; mandatory response providers, and commercial Fast Frequency Response
     providers of dynamic primary response (Ofgem's wording), under-delivered by approximately
     25% [reported].
D8   Ofgem 3.12: Ofgem does not believe that better response and reserve delivery would have been
     sufficient to prevent demand from being disconnected for this event.
