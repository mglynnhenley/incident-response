SECTION A — CIRCUIT AND NETWORK CONTEXT
A1  Eaton Socon–Wymondley Main: 400 kV [reported] double-circuit overhead line of just over 35 km [reported],
    from Eaton Socon substation (near St Neots, Cambridgeshire) to Wymondley Main substation (near Stevenage,
    Hertfordshire). Lattice steel towers carry two circuits, each with 3 [reported] conductors (phases) on one
    side of the tower, and an earth wire at the top of the tower.
A2  Planned outage on 9 August 2019: the Killingholme–Keadby circuit was out of service for planned
    maintenance; the NGET power quality monitor connected to it, close to Killingholme, was unavailable.

C1  2019-08-09T16:52:33.490+01:00 [reported]: fault inception. Single (blue) phase to earth fault on one Eaton
    Socon–Wymondley circuit: the middle conductor (blue phase) faulted to the earthed tower.
C2  Fault location: approximately 4.5 km [reported] along the circuit from the Wymondley Main end.
C3  Fault infeed: approximately 21 kA (RMS) [reported] from Wymondley Main; approximately
    7 kA (RMS) [reported] from Eaton Socon.
C4  Blue-phase voltage during the fault: estimated 50% [estimated] depression (ESO timeline); NGET: circa
    50% [reported] at the fault location, lasting 100 ms [reported] in the vicinity of the fault.
C5  2019-08-09T16:52:33.560+01:00 [reported]: Wymondley Main end opens to clear the fault; main protection
    operating time 70 ms [reported] after fault inception.
C6  2019-08-09T16:52:33.564+01:00 [reported]: Eaton Socon end opens to clear the fault; main protection
    operating time 74 ms [reported] after fault inception.
C7  Requirement cited by NGET, Grid Code Connection Condition CC.6.2.3.1.1, primary protection fault clearance
    times: 80 ms at 400 kV [reported]; 100 ms at 275 kV [reported]; 120 ms at 132 kV and below [reported].
C8  2019-08-09T16:52:53+01:00 [reported]: Eaton Socon–Wymondley circuit energised on Delayed Auto-Reclose
    (DAR), returning it to service about 20 seconds [reported] after the fault.
C9  DAR was available and in service on the circuit. After the reclose the network was in the same
    configuration as before the event; no further operational intervention or immediate site attendance was
    required.

SECTION G — POST-EVENT ASSESSMENTS (attributed later statements; not system records)
G1  NGET report (Appendix C to the ESO technical report of 6 September 2019): the network "operated as
    designed and in line with standards"; main protection cleared the fault within the 80 ms [reported]
    required in the Grid Code; the DAR system operated as expected and designed.
G2  NGET: one of the strikes is timed at the same time as the fault; "As such we can be confident that a
    lightning strike was the cause of the fault."
G3  NGET (power quality analysis and findings): the voltage depressions and durations were as expected for
    this type of fault; electrically further from the fault, voltage dips of 20% [reported] with
    80 ms [reported] duration were observed; the later small transient at Ryhall was most likely associated
    with reactive compensation switching in response to the fault, and was too small to register at Necton.
G4  NGET (steady state, harmonics and NPS): pre-fault and post-fault steady state voltages at Wymondley
    Main, Eaton Socon, Keadby and Killingholme 400 kV [reported] were within the SQSS operational limits of
    ±10% (420 kV to 380 kV) [reported]; harmonics were within the 3% [reported] limit of ER G5/4; NPS
    currents were within the 1.5% [reported] Grid Code limit.
G5  ESO technical report (6 September 2019): all measurements indicate that voltages stayed above the Fault
    Ride Through profile specified in the Grid Code and returned to within steady state limits after the
    event.
G6  ESO technical report: NGET, by analysing its protection data, confirmed that these lightning strikes
    were the cause of the circuit trip and reclose; the transmission system protection operated in line
    with the design and in compliance with the Grid Code requirements on fault clearance.
G7  Ofgem report (3 January 2020): NGET's evidence shows fault clearance times for the two circuit breakers
    under 75 ms [reported] and voltage within a range of 390 kV to 410 kV [reported] following the fault; the
    evidence suggests NGET's transmission assets performed as expected.
