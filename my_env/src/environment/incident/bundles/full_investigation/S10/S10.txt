PART 3 — ESO ANSWERS TO E3C QUESTIONS ON COMPLIANCE (Appendix M; based on the data available to the ESO on 28
August 2019)
Q01  Answer 43 (areas of concern about compliance testing with generators or OFTOs): after describing the
     compliance process, the ESO states: "We are not aware of concerns regarding the compliance testing
     process."
Q02  Answer 44: there have been no issues highlighted in the generation compliance process at Hornsea 1 or
     Little Barford in the past.
Q03  Answer 45: there are no direct tests of generator ride-through for faults and large or rapid frequency
     changes, which would require applying an intentional fault to the transmission system and could
     compromise its integrity. The asset owner performs simulation studies to demonstrate ride-through
     capability, and the ESO will not issue an Interim Operational Notice until it has reviewed these studies
     and determined that they are in line with Grid Code requirements.

PART 4 — OFGEM FINDINGS ON THE ESO'S COMPLIANCE CHECKS (Ofgem report, section 3 and executive summary)
G01  3.13: Ofgem has not found any evidence that the ESO failed to meet its requirements under the Grid Code
     in this area, and the ESO was not aware of any potential compliance issues with Hornsea 1 or Little
     Barford ahead of the event. However, the processes the ESO used to check their compliance do not appear
     sufficiently robust: the ESO relied significantly on self-certification by Hornsea 1 for the
     commissioning process as demonstration of its Grid Code compliance, despite the complexity of the
     connection, and after Little Barford's major refurbishment in 2011/12 relied on RWE's confirmation that
     the modifications had not affected compliance with the Grid Code requirements for generators to remain
     stable following network faults. No independent compliance testing or verification was carried out.
G02  3.14: Ofgem found limitations in the ESO's understanding of Hornsea 1's control system and of the
     interaction between its onshore and offshore arrangements, which impaired the ESO's view of Hornsea 1's
     performance after it was affected by the fault.
G03  3.15: the ESO allowed both Hornsea 1 and Little Barford to reconnect to the transmission system without
     taking adequate steps to determine the root cause of each failure. Hornsea 1 was reconnected and returned
     to service after the generator confirmed that the faulted equipment had been removed from service, but
     before the full root cause analysis had been completed, while it was still going through Grid Code
     compliance processes for commissioning generators.
G04  3.16: the Grid Code procedures the ESO is required to follow for checking generators' compliance may need
     to be clarified and strengthened; Ofgem expects the ESO to review the adequacy of the procedures it
     carries out and to flag potential compliance concerns to Ofgem.
G05  Executive summary: some licensees do not appear to have met their licence and code requirements, and the
     event highlights the importance of robust industry compliance processes. Ofgem has not identified any
     failures by the ESO to meet its requirements which contributed to the outage.

PART 5 — OFGEM FINDINGS ON PERFORMANCE AGAINST REQUIREMENTS (Ofgem report, section 3 and executive summary)
P01  Hornsea 1, 3.20: Hornsea 1's two fully operational modules deloaded automatically from around
     737 MW [estimated] to zero after the lightning strike, whilst its remaining module continued to generate
     at 62 MW [reported]; this occurred after the network fault on the Eaton Socon - Wymondley circuits had
     been rectified.
P02  Hornsea 1, 3.22: modelling that Ørsted carried out with its equipment manufacturer before 9 August
     suggested performance issues with the voltage control system with Hornsea 1 operating at its full planned
     1,200 MW [reported] capacity; these issues were not discussed with the ESO. The manufacturer proposed a
     software update, which Ørsted told Ofgem it had planned to carry out on 13 August but implemented on 10
     August, immediately after the event. The issue on 9 August occurred when the wind farm reached
     799 MW [reported]; prior simulations and discussions had not foreseen such issues at this capacity, nor
     that the wind farm would become unstable and de-load even at full load.
P03  Hornsea 1, 3.23: Hornsea 1 did not notify the ESO directly when the wind farm deloaded by
     737 MW [reported]. It then began starting up two of its modules (temporarily) without any coordination
     with the ESO, while the ESO was attempting to respond to the generation losses it was aware of.
P04  Hornsea 1, 3.24: the Grid Code requirements on generators' responses to network faults, known as Fault
     Ride Through requirements, require that generators remain connected and transiently stable following a
     fault on the transmission system, with power output recovering to at least 90% [reported] within
     0.5 seconds [reported] (Ofgem cites Connection Condition CC.6.3.15.1). Hornsea 1 has acknowledged that it
     did not meet this requirement, having deloaded following the fault. Hornsea 1 has also accepted that it
     did not meet the Grid Code requirement to have an overall voltage control system which appropriately
     dampens or limits swings (Ofgem cites CC.A.7.2.5.2, Voltage Control (Oscillations)).
P05  Little Barford, 3.26: Fault Ride Through requirements are fundamental to the security and resilience of
     the power system; Little Barford's generators did not continue providing power to the system following
     the lightning strike, and this contributed to the power outage.
P06  Little Barford, 3.28 to 3.29: the steam turbine tripped initially because of a discrepancy between its
     three independent speed sensors that exceeded the tolerance of the control system; the root cause of the
     discrepancy has not been established. One gas turbine was shut down automatically on excessive pressure
     in the steam bypass and the other was tripped manually by plant operators; the root cause of these high
     pressure conditions has not been established. Given the sequence of events and in the absence of an
     established root cause, Ofgem's preliminary view is that the steam turbine's anomalous speed readings and
     the resulting trip were due to the transmission network fault. RWE Generation has acknowledged the role
     it played in contributing to the power outage by not continuing to provide power to the system following
     the fault.
P07  DNOs, 3.36: most DNOs would appear to have met the Grid Code requirements regarding LFDD, but Ofgem found
     issues with essential services being disconnected, areas for improvement in the LFDD arrangements, and
     concerning evidence that some DNOs disconnected distributed generation via LFDD that was providing
     frequency response or reserve services. Most DNOs reconnected customers as required, but Ofgem found
     issues with two DNOs which could potentially have jeopardised the recovery of the system.
P08  DNOs, 3.43: DNOs' reconnection of demand following Stage 1 of LFDD generally performed as expected.
     However, Eastern Power Networks plc and South Eastern Power Networks plc (both owned by UK Power
     Networks) reconnected sites without being told to do so by the ESO, and acknowledge that they do not
     appear to have technically met the requirements of the Grid Code (Ofgem cites Grid Code Operation Code
     6.6.4). These instances were caused by the configuration of the DNOs' operational systems. The system
     frequency was successfully restored to 50 Hz [reported] following the LFDD activation; the premature
     demand reconnection could potentially have jeopardised this outcome.
P09  Executive summary: two DNOs (Eastern Power Networks plc and South Eastern Power Networks plc, owned by UK
     Power Networks) reconnected customers in England before the required instructions from the ESO, and this
     could have jeopardised recovery of the system.
P10  DNOs, 3.44: SP Distribution erroneously disconnected 22 MW [reported] in Scotland when system frequency
     fell below 48.8 Hz [reported] and reconnected customers without informing the ESO. As part of the SP
     Transmission network area it is not required by the Grid Code to disconnect demand until frequency
     reaches 48.5 Hz [reported]; the disconnection was due to an incorrect setting in its LFDD equipment.
     Since it was not responding to an LFDD event, Ofgem states that it does not appear to have contravened
     the Grid Code in its disconnection or prompt reconnection of demand without informing the ESO.
P11  NGET, 3.17 to 3.18: NGET's evidence shows fault clearance times for the two circuit breakers under
     75 ms [reported] and voltage remaining within a range of 390 kV [reported] to 410 kV [reported] following
     the fault; the evidence suggests that NGET's transmission assets performed as expected based on its Grid
     Code and SQSS requirements.
P12  Distributed generation, 3.31 to 3.32: some generators with capacities greater than 5 MW [reported] were
     reported to have de-loaded during the event, for reasons still to be determined precisely; their RoCoF
     protection settings should have been changed following Distribution Code modifications made in 2014. It
     is possible that the frequency protection settings on some generators were not changed in line with
     Distribution Code requirements.
